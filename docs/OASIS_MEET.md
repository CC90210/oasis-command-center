# Oasis Meet

Our own video meetings (CC, 2026-10-17). A host starts a meeting from the **Schedule** page (the Meet button), sends
the link, and people join in the browser with no account: `oasisai.work/meet/abc-defg-hjk`. One host's browser (any
host in the call) records each speaker's audio; OASIS's PC transcribes it on the CPU and keeps the audio; the Command
Center keeps the transcript. No meeting vendor bill: media runs on Cloudflare Realtime inside its free tier.

## How it works

| Piece | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Rooms and people | `lib/meet/rooms.ts`, `lib/meet/participants.ts`, migrations `bravo__230`, `bravo__233` | `meet_rooms` (the link code, title, host, scheduled/live/ended, record on/off, Schedule event, lead, and `recorder_participant_id`: the one browser recording it) and `meet_participants` (one row per person per join: role, name, consent, SFU session, published tracks, left/removed). Every query names the room's tenant; the code lookup is the one read not by workspace, because the code is what a guest holds. |
| Who may do what | `lib/meet/access.ts` | Host: the room's creator, or an owner/admin of its workspace by base role. Guest: anyone with the link. A guest's join sets an HMAC participant token (`MEET_TOKEN_SECRET`) in an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookie scoped to `/api/meet/<code>`; every call re-reads the participant row, so leaving, removal and an ended room apply at once. In a recorded room everyone but its creator, a second host too, ticks the recording notice. A member's meeting list shows the meetings they made; an owner/admin's shows all of the workspace's. |
| The room API | `lib/meet/handlers.ts`, `app/api/meet/[code]/*` | `join`, `state` (polled every 2 s), `sfu` (session, publish, pull, renegotiate, close), `leave`, `host` (remove, end, delete). Public at the middleware; each route checks same origin, the code, a rate limit, then the token or the host's session. |
| Host management | `app/api/calendar/meet` | List and make rooms (signed in). A meeting with a time also goes on the host's Schedule through the Schedule's own write path (`lib/meet/schedule.ts`, Shabbat lock included). The list says `recordingAvailable`; where it is false a room never records, so no guest is asked to agree to a recording nobody makes. |
| Deleting a meeting | `host` action `delete`, `deleteMeetRoom` | A host only. In this order: a meeting still going is ended and its media cut; the recording is deleted (`deleteMeetRecording`: the transcript, the relay, the PC's copy); the creator's Schedule entry is removed (never another person's calendar); then the room's rows. If the recording cannot be deleted, the meeting is kept, so nothing is orphaned. A meeting a lead booked is refused here (409 `booked_meeting`): cancelling it from the lead deletes it with the booking's own calendar entries and invite. |
| Booked meetings | `lib/meet/links.ts`, `rooms.ts`, `handlers.ts`, `guest-name.ts` | A booking (the `/book` page, an offer form's inline booking, a rep's founder meeting) makes the lead their own room: `createMeetRoom` with the slot as `startsAt`, the lead as `leadId`, CC as its creator, and no Schedule entry of Meet's (the booking writes its own). What a booking calls: `meetRoomUrl` (the one link its invite, emails and calendar file carry), `rescheduleMeetRoom` (the same link at the new time, for any meeting not over), `deleteMeetRoom(liveMeetDeps(), ...)` (a cancel, server-side) and `meetMediaReady(liveMeetDeps())` (when it is false, the booking gives a Google Meet link instead, so a booking never fails because Meet is down). The room page fills in the lead's first name for them (`meetGuestFirstName`: their own record in the room's workspace, first name only, since anyone holding the link sees it; never when the page could not tell who is looking, since a host whose session lookup failed would otherwise join as the lead) and says when the call starts, in their own time zone. The Meet panel shows a booked meeting as "Booked by a lead. Cancel it from their page." instead of Delete. |
| Media | `lib/meet/sfu.ts`, `lib/meet/ice.ts` | Cloudflare Realtime SFU through our API (the app secret never reaches a browser). A pull names a participant and a track; the server allows only what someone else in the same room publishes now. TURN credentials are minted per join (STUN only when unset). Remove and end close every track on the affected sessions at the SFU, sent and received. |
| The browser | `lib/meet/room-client.ts`, `components/meet/*`, `app/meet/[code]` | One RTCPeerConnection per person; each SDP exchange finishes before the next change. The page asks for the recording tick before the camera (Quebec Law 25: notice before collection). `next.config.js` lets `/meet/*` use the camera and microphone; every other page keeps them off. |
| Recording and transcripts | `lib/meet/recorder.ts`, `lib/meet/recording*.ts`, BEA `scripts/meet/` | Each speaker's audio, recorded separately in one host's browser (the room's recorder), uploaded in 10 s chunks to a private R2 relay, transcribed on OASIS's PC by a separate runner (faster-whisper on CPU), saved there with the audio; the relay copy is deleted once the PC has it. Only OASIS workspaces record. |

## Switching it on (one time)

1. Cloudflare API token: **Realtime > Realtime Admin** on the OASIS account (the API reference calls it `Calls Write`;
   it is the only permission that creates an SFU app or a TURN key). Then BEA `scripts/integrations/cf_realtime_tool.py
   create` makes both and stores their secrets without showing them.
2. Worker secrets (names in BEA `config/cloudflare/manifests/oasis-command-center.json`; push with
   `wrangler_tool.py secrets-push --app oasis-command-center --only <KEY>`): `MEET_TOKEN_SECRET` (done 2026-10-17),
   `CF_REALTIME_APP_ID`, `CF_REALTIME_APP_SECRET`, `CF_TURN_KEY_ID`, `CF_TURN_KEY_API_TOKEN`. Without the SFU app every
   media call answers 503 `meet_not_configured`.
3. Recording: migration `bravo__231`, an R2 lifecycle rule on `meet-relay/`, the Meet runner as its own Windows task
   on OASIS's PC, and the `oasis-cc-cron` Worker deployed so `/api/cron/meet-relay` runs.

## Limits, on purpose

- Eight people per room at most (one statement with the join, so it cannot be overshot). Guests hold seven places at most, so a leaked link filled with guests never locks the host out of the room they need to be in to remove them. A place stays held until its person has been silent for 5 minutes, because until then they can come back to it: a newcomer never takes the place of someone whose laptop slept. The same statement takes a place only in a room still there and not ended, so a join that lands as the host ends or deletes the meeting is told so and leaves no row (no guest's name outlives a deleted meeting).
- A live room ends itself when no host has been seen for 10 minutes, but only after a call happened in it: two different people in the room at once, one of them an authorized host (CC is the owner; one person in two tabs is not a call). A host who opens a meeting alone, to test or early, leaves it waiting for its guests. A call nobody polls any more (every tab closed) ends when a host opens the Meet panel, and in the recording relay's sweep, so its recording is handed over. Backstop: a room nobody has been in for 12 hours, whose start (if it has one) is 12 hours past, ends as `expired`.
- Someone not heard from for 5 minutes has left (their session is cut; coming back is a new join, through the cap), so a browser that stops polling but keeps its connection is never left listening unseen. Their page says "You were disconnected from the meeting." with Rejoin; nothing rejoins them by itself, because a new join in a recorded room needs the notice ticked again. Less than that (a laptop that slept a moment, a locked phone, a short outage) is only a gap in the People list. A poll that hangs is given up after 10 seconds, so it never stops the polls after it; every other call to the room (the join, each SFU step, a host action) after 20, so no screen waits on one for good. The deadline is a plain timer and AbortController, as the recorder's uploads use, so every browser has it (`AbortSignal.timeout` is missing in Safari before 16). Leave ends the call at once, camera and all, and tells the room after: once a join still on its way has answered, since only that answer carries the cookie a leave needs, so the place the join makes is freed.
- A meeting made for a time counts a call only if it reaches that time (less 10 minutes): hosts trying the room out early never end the link its guests were sent.
- A reconnect replaces the SFU session only once the old one is cut, so the browser tries six times when the video service refuses it (three for anything else) before it gives up. The new session is written to the row first and checked after: a leave, removal, end or delete that landed while the video service made it is caught and the session cut, and a leave cuts the session its row holds once the leave is written, so no session outlives its person or its room. The others see each person's session as a tag that changes on a reconnect, and pull their tracks anew; a screen being shared goes out again on the new session, and one stopped during the reconnect (the browser's own "Stop sharing" works in every phase) does not.
- A camera, microphone or screen asked for and answered after it stopped being wanted (asked again, turned off, the call left or failed, even with the join then tried again) is stopped, never held: no light left on.
- A browser prompt nobody answers never locks the pre-join screen: "Join without your camera" (or microphone, or both) stops waiting and joins with what is already on; "Join now" stays off while the browser is still answering, so nobody joins without the camera they just allowed.
- "Recorded" is shown in the call only while the browser recording it is in the call (heard from within 5 minutes, so a late poll does not hide it).
- End asks the room to end before the recorder stops: an End that fails keeps the meeting going and recorded.
- Media is cut at the SFU on leave, remove, end and delete. A row keeps its session id until the cut goes through; a failed one is retried from the room's polls (after the answer, at most every 30 seconds per room) for an hour, and by Delete. Delete never waits on an SFU that does not answer. A session the SFU no longer has (404) or whose browser has gone (410 "Session appears to be disconnected", which every Leave meets, and every reconnect after a dropped network) counts as cut: no media flows through it, and it can be neither read nor closed.
- One authorized host browser records a room. The first host's browser to find nobody recording takes it, in one conditional statement, so two host sessions joining at once never both do. It keeps it while it polls; a host coming back, or joining on a second device, never takes it back. When the recorder leaves, is removed or goes unheard for 30 seconds, the next authorized host's poll takes over: its recorder starts new segments, with every voice already in the call. The chunk route takes audio only from the recorder, with two exceptions that may send their last chunks for 10 minutes after: the recorder just taken over (even one that went silent rather than leave), and any browser that left or was removed. Any other browser still in the call gets 423. A hand-over can repeat up to a timeslice (10 s) of overlap but leaves no gap. A takeover after a crash loses the silent spell, about 30 seconds. Every recorder takes its segment times from the room's clock (the server's time on the join and poll answers, read from the quickest one), so recording segments line up whatever their own clocks say. Known limits, for e9's follow-up: a hand-over drops the previous recorder's failed and errored counts from the panel's "missing" floor, and a takeover in the last minutes before End can race the PC's claim. If the recording passes on twice while the first recorder is still retrying uploads, only the latest one taken over is exempt.
- One tab per browser in a room (`MeetTabs`: a Web Lock per room, which the browser also releases when a tab closes or crashes, and a BroadcastChannel). Two tabs of one browser share the room's cookie, so they are one participant, and would cut each other's media and record the room twice.
  - A second tab is told "This meeting is already open in another tab of this browser." It sends the room nothing.
  - "Join here instead" moves the meeting to the new tab. The first tab ends with "You moved this meeting to another tab." and tells the room nothing, so the new tab carries on as the same participant, with the same place, recording and notice tick.
  - A first tab still joining hands over only once its join has answered, so the new tab joins with the cookie that answer set and never makes a second participant. The new tab waits 25 s, longer than a join's own 20 s deadline.
  - An ask more than 3 s old is ignored. It reached a tab that was frozen meanwhile, such as a phone's background tab, and its asker has given up, so a call is never ended for a hand-over nobody is waiting to take.
  - The tab handing over lets go only once its SFU steps (session, publish, pull) have answered, since a late one would act on the session and tracks of the participant the new tab now is. A tab asked while its join is on its way hands over before setting up any media, so at most one step is ever waited for: 3 s for the ask plus 20 s for that step fits inside the new tab's 25 s.
  - A call ended while it is connecting (moved, left, removed) is never brought back to live.
  - If the new tab's join then fails, nobody is in the call until "Try again", which comes back as the same participant. The two tabs share one participant, so they can never be in the call at once.
  - Known limit: closing the tab in the call while another is waiting to take over races the closing tab's leave beacon against the new tab's join, since the browser lets go of the lock at once. A leave landing after the join reads "You were disconnected" in the new tab, and Rejoin brings it back. A per-tab id on the participant row would make the late leave harmless, as a follow-up if it shows up in practice.
  - Only the tab in the room sends a Leave or the page's close beacon, so a second tab can never end the first one's place.
  - Leave lets go of the room only once the room has heard it.
  - A browser without Web Locks keeps tabs apart as before, which is not at all.
- Remove is not a ban: the person can come back with the link from a new browser window (there is no account to ban).
- Rate limits are per Worker isolate (a slowdown for guessing links); the participant cap is the hard ceiling. Up to eight people behind one office address fit the poll limit.
