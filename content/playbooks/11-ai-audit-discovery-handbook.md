# Free AI Audit: Discovery Call Handbook

> How to run the discovery call that a Free AI Audit is built on. The audit can only be as good as what you learn on this call: every figure in it comes from the client, from a source we can show, or from a calculation on those two. This handbook and the live-call screen use the same questions, word for word.

---

## What the call is for

- **Understand the business as the owner sees it.** Their services, their customers, how work flows, where time goes.
- **Collect the figures the audit needs**, with how the client knows each one: lead volume, reply time, close rate, typical job value, admin hours, the cost of an hour of staff time.
- **Find the one problem they most want fixed.** The roadmap starts there.
- **Set expectations.** A free written audit follows, with no obligation.

What the call is **not** for: selling, quoting, or diagnosing. You are listening. The audit does the recommending, after research.

## Consent: always first

Read this, or say it in your own words without changing its meaning:

> "Before we start: may I record this call? We transcribe it and analyse it with AI services that work for OASIS, only to prepare your free audit."

- **If they say yes:** tick consent on the live-call screen. Oasis Meet also shows its own recording notice, and each guest agrees on joining.
- **If they say no:** that is completely fine. Say "No problem, I'll take notes as we go," and do not record. Then ask one more thing: "Is it all right if we use AI tools to help analyse my notes for your audit?"
  - Yes: record "recording: no, AI analysis: yes" in the profile. Your notes feed the automated research and writing.
  - No: record both as no. You write the profile and the audit yourself, and the system will not send their information to any AI service. Gate 1 shows this before you authorize.
- **If they hesitate:** explain what happens to the recording (it is transcribed, used only for their audit, and never shared) and let them choose. Never push.

Mentioning AI here is a disclosure, not a pitch. It does not break the rule that the conversation is about their business first.

## The questions

The questions come in eight groups. **Essential** questions are the ones the audit cannot be written without. **Optional** ones deepen it when the call has room. You do not need to ask them in order or word for word; you need the answers. The live-call screen shows which answers are still missing.

### Opening and consent

1. **Essential.** "Before we start: may I record this call? We transcribe it and analyse it with AI services that work for OASIS, only to prepare your free audit."
   - Why: recording and AI analysis need the client's clear yes.
2. **Essential.** "Here is how I would like to use our time: I will ask about how the business runs today, where time goes, and what you want to change. You get a free written audit afterwards, with no obligation. Does that work?"
   - Why: it sets the frame. A conversation about their business, a free audit, no pitch.
   - Follow up: "Is there anything you specifically want us to look at?"

### Business context

3. **Essential.** "In your own words, what does the business do, and who is it for?"
   - Why: the audit opens with our understanding of the business, in their words.
   - Follow up: "Who is your best kind of customer?" "Which part of the work do people come to you for most?"
4. **Essential.** "What are the main services or products, and how does the business make its money?"
   - Why: recommendations have to fit how revenue actually arrives.
   - Follow up: "Is most of it one-off jobs, repeat customers, or subscriptions?"
5. *Optional.* "How many people work in the business, and where do you serve customers?"
   - Why: size decides which tools are realistic.
6. **Essential.** "What are you trying to achieve this year, and in the longer run?"
   - Why: the roadmap is ordered by what they want, not by what we sell.

### Daily operations

7. **Essential.** "Which tasks repeat every week and eat the most time?"
   - Why: repetitive work is where automation pays back. Hours feed the estimates.
   - Follow up: "Roughly how many hours a week does that take?" "Who does it?" "How do you know that number: a guess, or records?"
8. **Essential.** "Where do you copy information from one place to another by hand?"
   - Why: re-keying between systems is the most common and most fixable waste.
9. *Optional.* "Where do things get stuck, or fall through the cracks between people?"
   - Follow up: "Can you tell me about the last time that happened?"

### Technology

10. **Essential.** "Which software do you use to run the business day to day?"
    - Why: we build on what they already pay for before suggesting anything new.
    - Follow up: "Roughly what does it cost a month? Only if you know."
11. **Essential.** "Which of those talk to each other, and which do not?"
    - Why: disconnected systems are where integrations help most.
12. *Optional.* "What frustrates you most about your current tools?"

### Sales and marketing

13. **Essential.** "Where do new customers come from, and roughly how many enquiries a month?"
    - Why: lead volume is the base of most revenue estimates.
    - Follow up: "Is that a guess, or do you track it?"
14. **Essential.** "Walk me through what happens when a new enquiry comes in."
    - Why: the path from enquiry to booking is where most lost customers are lost.
    - Follow up: "How long until the first reply, usually?" "What happens after hours or at weekends?"
15. **Essential.** "Out of every ten enquiries, how many become customers? And what is a typical job worth?"
    - Why: close rate and job value turn faster replies and saved time into money, as a range.
16. *Optional, not for individual productivity audits.* "Where do you lose people: missed calls, slow quotes, no follow-up?"
    - Follow up: "How many calls a week go to voicemail, roughly?"

### Financial and performance metrics

17. *Optional.* "Which costs would you most like to bring down?"
18. **Essential.** "What does an hour of your team's time cost the business, roughly?"
    - Why: it turns hours saved into a cost figure. If they would rather not say, the estimate stays in hours. That is fine.
19. *Optional.* "Which numbers do you watch to know the business is doing well?"

### Priorities and decision-making

20. **Essential.** "If the right technology were ready tomorrow, what would you fix first?"
    - Why: the first roadmap item should be the one they already want.
21. *Optional.* "What has stopped you fixing this before?"
    - Why: past attempts show the real constraints: time, money, trust, or a bad experience.
22. **Essential.** "Who else is involved in a decision like this, and is there a timeline we should know about?"
    - Follow up: "When would you want something like this working?"
23. *Optional, only if the conversation allows.* "Is there a budget range you have in mind? Only if you are comfortable saying."
    - Why: it shapes how the roadmap is phased. It never sets the price: prices come from the catalogue, after the call.

### Close and next steps

24. **Essential.** "Here is what happens next: we research the business, write your free audit, and send you a private link. Is email the best way to reach you?"
    - Follow up: "Is there anything we did not cover that you want in the audit?"

## Getting real answers

**Vague answers.** "A lot of leads" is not a figure. Ask for a range: "Would you say closer to 10 a month, or closer to 100?" Then ask how they know. Record exactly what they said and how sure they were.

**Figures need a source.** Every number you capture is Client-reported unless you saw evidence (a report, a dashboard, an invoice). If they show you something on screen, note what it was: that is what lets the audit call it a Verified fact.

**Quantify the problem in their units.** Hours a week, enquiries a month, days to reply. Do not convert to money on the call. The audit does that, as a range, with its assumptions written down.

**Follow the pain, briefly.** When they describe a problem, ask what it costs them: "What happens when that follow-up is missed?" One follow-up is usually enough. You are mapping, not dramatising.

**Spotting inefficiencies.** Listen for these:
- the same information typed twice;
- a person waiting on another person to pass something on;
- enquiries answered "when I get to it";
- after-hours enquiries going nowhere;
- a tool they pay for but do not use;
- the owner doing work a system could do.

**Silence is fine.** After a question, wait. The best answers come after the pause.

## The rules

- **No prices on this call.** If they ask, say: "I will not guess at a number before we have looked properly. The audit includes a proposal with exact prices." This settles the price conflict in the training material for this call: no rep and no founder quotes on a discovery call.
- **The business comes first; AI comes later.** Ask about their process before any technology. Talk about AI only if they raise it, and even then briefly: the audit will say where it fits. This settles the "AI on the first call" conflict for this call, the same way the live objection catalogue already does.
- **No promises.** Never say a result will happen ("you will double your leads"). The audit shows estimated ranges with their assumptions.
- **No statistics you cannot source.** Not "most businesses lose 30% of calls". If you know a real figure, the audit will cite it properly.
- **Never take a password, a login or a card number.** If they start reading one out, stop them politely: "Please do not share that on a call. If we work together, access is set up securely through our Connections page." Write "credential mentioned" in that question's notes. When the transcript is added, the Command Center takes out what it recognizes; open **View lines** and check that part of the call yourself before you authorize anything.
- **No pressure.** "Not now" is a fine answer. So is "this is not a fit". Thank them either way.

## Two example calls (fictional)

**A plumbing company, owner on the phone.**
1. Consent, then the agenda.
2. "In your own words, what does the business do?" Residential repairs and renovations, two vans.
3. Repetitive tasks: the owner writes every quote at night, about six hours a week (a guess). You ask how they know; they say it is "most evenings".
4. Enquiries: "maybe 40 a month", mostly calls, many missed while on a job. You ask: "How many go to voicemail in a week?" They do not know. That stays a gap; the audit will say so.
5. Close rate and job value: "half of the ones we quote, a typical job is around two thousand". Record both as Client-reported.
6. First fix: "answering calls while I am under a sink."
7. Decision: the owner and their partner, no deadline. Close.

What the audit can then do: estimate the value of missed calls as a range, from their figures, and recommend call capture first, because that is what they asked for.

**A bookkeeping practice, three staff, video call.**
1. Consent. They decline recording; you take notes and say so.
2. Services: monthly bookkeeping and year-end for small businesses.
3. Manual work: chasing clients for receipts by email, re-keying bank lines into a spreadsheet. The office manager says "a full day a week" and shows a timesheet on screen. You note it: that figure can be Verified, with the timesheet as its evidence.
4. Tools: a cloud ledger, a shared inbox, a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet talks to nothing.
5. Hourly cost: they would rather not say. Fine: the estimate stays in hours.
6. First fix: receipt collection. Decision makers: the two partners, at their next monthly meeting.

## Right after the call

- Mark the call completed. A call held in the lead's Oasis Meet room attaches its own transcript when Meet has it ready, if every guest agreed to the recording on joining and the workspace owner recorded the client's answer about AI analysis on the opening question. Anything else waits under Transcripts, with the reason and an alert, for the owner to attach by hand, saying what the client agreed to: no answer recorded (or one another founder recorded), part of the audio lost, a guest removed or no guest heard, or a call that ended before the engagement was opened. For a call held elsewhere, paste or upload the transcript, or work from your notes.
- Check the discovery profile. Every field shows its label and where it came from. Correct anything the transcript got wrong.
- Then authorize processing (Gate 1). Nothing is researched or written before you do. The operating handbook covers everything after this point.
